# Protecting Grout After an Installation - This Old House

Skip to content

Grout plays a crucial role in tile installations, but it’s often overlooked when it comes to maintenance and upkeep. Protecting your grout helps tiled surfaces last longer and look beautiful.

In this guide, we’ll explore the importance of grout protection, methods to safeguard your grout, and how to maintain it for years to come. Whether you’re dealing with bathroom tiles, kitchen backsplashes, or flooring, these tips will help you keep your grout looking fresh and clean.

Understanding Grout and Its Vulnerabilities

Before diving into protection methods, it’s important to understand grout and why it’s susceptible to damage. What Is Grout?

Grout is a mixture of cement, water, and sometimes sand that fills the spaces between tiles. It holds tiles in place, prevents water from seeping behind tiles, and makes the surface look polished and complete. Grout is durable but porous, which means it can be vulnerable to stains, moisture, and bacteria.

Pro Tip: David Goodman, the tile contractor for This Old House’s Nantucket project, explains: “Not only does grout fill the voids, it makes the floor, wall, or countertop stronger by bonding the tiles together and preventing the edges of a tile from chipping and cracking.” Common Causes of Grout Damage

Several factors can contribute to grout damage: Foot traffic Harsh cleaning chemicals Improper installation Moisture penetration Mold and mildew growth

Pro Tip: Joe Ferrante, tiling contractor and regular on This Old House projects, explains: “Grout typically cracks for one of two reasons: the floor framing is deflecting or there’s an installation flaw.” The Importance of Sealing Grout

Sealing grout is one of the most effective ways to protect it from damage. This involves applying a protective layer that repels water, stains, and dirt.

Pro Tip: Tile contractor Joe Ferrante, who installed the stone floor tiles at the Winchester project, recommends: “After the stone is laid, seal it with a penetrating sealer, sometimes called stone impregnator. Then fill the joints-and only the joints-using a grout bag, which you can get at masonry supply stores.” Benefits of Sealed Grout

Sealing your grout offers numerous advantages: Easier cleaning and maintenance Enhanced appearance of tiled surfaces Extended lifespan of grout and tiles Prevention of mold and mildew growth Stain resistance

Pro Tip: Christie, a grout specialist, explains a key benefit of modern sealed grout: “It’s much easier to clean than standard grout. And we have a fungicide in it to keep it from mildewing, which, again, is important in your steam area.” This built-in protection means “you don’t have to do the toothbrush business when you’re cleaning.” How Often Should You Seal Grout?

How often you seal your grout depends on the type of grout, location, and usage:

Seal high-traffic areas every six to 12 months for high-traffic areas

Seal low-traffic areas every two to three years for low-traffic areas

Seal new grout installation immediately after it cures

If water no longer beads on the grout’s surface, it’s time to reseal. Choosing the Right Grout Sealer

Various grout sealers are available, each with its own characteristics and benefits. The main types of grout sealers are penetrating and non-penetrating:

Penetrating sealers: These soak into the grout, providing invisible protection without changing the appearance. Be careful when using penetrating sealers on older grout, as they can “lock in” existing stains.

Non-penetrating sealers: These create a visible layer on top of the grout. Many homeowners like the shiny finish, but we caution against using non-penetrating sealers for wet areas such as bathrooms. The membrane coating can make it hard for water to evaporate, encouraging mold growth. When choosing a grout sealer, consider: Aesthetic preferences Desired level of protection Ease of application The location (indoor or outdoor) The type of tile and grout

We recommend a clear silicone sealer for most homeowners. This penetrating sealer is effective and easy to apply. Step-by-Step Guide To Sealing Grout

Let the sealer cure according to the manufacturer’s instructions. This typically takes 24 to 72 hours. Avoid walking on the sealed area or exposing it to water during this time.

You can test your grout sealer by dripping a few drops of water onto it. If the water beads up on the surface, it’s cured. If the water soaks in and darkens the grout, it needs more time.

Pro Tip: Temperature and weather conditions significantly impact curing time. Sealers require between 24 and 36 hours of dry, warm weather to fully cure, and temperatures must remain above 45 degrees during the curing period for best results.

Alternative Methods for Protecting Grout

Sealing is highly effective. However, there are also other protection methods, which can be used with or instead of sealing. Grout Additives

Grout additives are mixed into the grout during installation. They provide several advantages: Built-in water resistance Enhanced durability Improved flexibility More consistent color

Proper maintenance and regular cleaning without harsh chemicals can help keep your grout looking fresh. Regular Cleaning Techniques To maintain sealed grout:

Sweep or vacuum regularly to remove loose dirt

Use a pH-neutral cleaner for routine cleaning

Avoid abrasive scrubbers that can wear down the sealer

Rinse thoroughly with clean water after cleaning Avoiding Harsh Chemicals

Harsh chemicals can damage both the sealer and the grout itself. Avoid: Bleach-based cleaners Acidic cleaners (such as vinegar) Ammonia-based products

Instead, opt for mild, pH-neutral cleaners specifically designed for tile and grout. Troubleshooting Common Grout Issues

Even with proper protection, grout can sometimes develop issues. Knowing how to address these problems can help maintain your tiled surfaces. Dealing with Stains Try these steps to clean stained grout:

Mix equal parts baking soda and hydrogen peroxide to form a paste.

Apply the paste to the stain and let it sit for several hours.

Scrub gently with a soft brush and rinse thoroughly.

For stubborn stains, consider a commercial grout cleaner. Repairing Cracked or Damaged Grout

Follow these steps to repair damaged grout:

Remove the damaged grout using a grout saw or removal tool.

Clean the area thoroughly and allow it to dry.

Apply new grout, matching the color to the existing grout.

Once cured, seal the new grout to protect it. Advanced Grout Protection Techniques

You can combine advanced techniques with traditional sealing to provide extra protection and longevity to your grout, especially in high-use areas such as bathrooms and kitchens. These methods can also help reduce maintenance needs. Applying Grout Colorant

Grout colorant is a two-in-one solution that stains and seals grout. It has a number of uses: Offer better stain resistance Provide a water-resistant barrier Restore or change the grout color

You can use colorant on both freshly-cured and old grout, making this a versatile option. Steam Cleaning

Steam cleaning is an effective way to deep-clean grout lines without harsh chemicals. The steam’s high temperature provides a number of benefits: Kill mold and bacteria Remove embedded dirt and grime Revitalize appearance

Most hardware stores rent steam cleaners, making this a practical DIY option. Using Antimicrobial Additives

Antimicrobial additives can be added to grout during installation to help prevent mold and mildew. They provide benefits that are particularly useful in areas prone to moisture: Long-lasting protection Reduced need for frequent cleaning Safe usage in damp conditions Professional Grout Maintenance Services
