Edging your lawn is a great way to clean up the sides of flowerbeds or walkways, and will make your lawn look neat and well-maintained. While there are both mechanical and manual edging tools, you can also edge your lawn using a shovel or lawn trimmer. Regardless of what method you choose, edging your lawn without a lawn edger is a breeze as long as you take your time and follow the right steps. Steps Part 1 Part 1 of 2: Creating an Edge with a Shovel Download Article 1

Make a straight line with 2 wooden sticks and string. Set 2 wooden sticks into the ground 1–2 inches (2.5–5.1 cm) from the edge of your flower bed or walkway. Then, tie a piece of string to each stick to form a straight line that will become the edge.[1]XResearch source

Setting the line before you start edging the lawn will keep your work neater.

You can also try to edge by eye, but it may look sloppy. 2

Use spray paint to create the edge line instead of using string. If the edge is curvy, as could be the case with a flower bed, using lawn spray paint is a better idea than using multiple sticks and strings. Shake the can of spray paint and make an edge 1–2 inches (2.5–5.1 cm) from the edge of your flower bed or walkway. Try to stay as parallel with the edge of the lawn as you can with the paint.[2]XResearch source Advertisement 3

Place the blade of a flat-edge shovel along the line. Place the shovel on a 90-degree angle along the edge that you marked out. Make sure that the shovel lines up with the line that you created before you push down.[3]XResearch source 4

Press down on the top of the shovel with your foot. Push the shovel 2 inches (5.1 cm) into the turf. Once the shovel is in the ground, press down on the top handle to push up the dirt and free the grass.[4]XResearch source

2 inches (5.1 cm) is a good depth to set your edge and will prevent you from cutting into wires or pipes.[5]XResearch source

Call 811 to find out about the utility lines so that you do not damage them by mistake.[6]XExpert SourceKeith Souza

Cut the grass along the line, then remove it. Move down the line with the shovel, continuing to dig 2 inches (5.1 cm) into the turf. When you reach the end of the line, use your shovel to dig up the freed grass and remove it from the area. You should now have a straight edge running parallel to your walkway or flower bed.[8]XResearch source 6

Sweep the area clean with a broom. Sweep around the edge you created to remove excess dirt and grass that you pulled up while digging. If you want an even cleaner look, you can use a lawn trimmer to further clean up the edge.[9]XResearch source

You can maintain the look of your edge with a shovel alone.

This is especially important because you'll be inverting the natural position of the lawn trimmer or weed whacker.
