What To Know Before Buying an Infrared Heater

How do you narrow down the best infrared heater to supplement your home’s heating system when there are so many different brands and models out there? A well-made space heater should ideally provide the output needed to create a comfortable ambient temperature without creating an eyesore or tripping hazard. Here’s a rundown of the core details to put on your checklist when shopping for a new space or wall heater. Heat Output

The heat output for portable space heaters is measured by British Thermal Units (Btu). While you don’t need a full science lesson to understand how BTUs relate to space heaters, it does help to know that one BTU represents the quantity of heat required to raise the temperature of a single pound of liquid water by 1 degree Fahrenheit (F) at approximately 39 degrees Fahrenheit. All shoppers need to know is that a higher BTU number equals a more powerful space heater. Most electric heaters sold in the United States are 1,500-watt units. That means that they generate roughly 5,100 BTUs per hour.

When shopping for a space heater, pay attention to what the manufacturer shares regarding the heating capacity of the unit in terms of square footage. Infrared space heaters that can heat up to 1,500 square feet of space can be ideal if you’re looking for supplemental heat for a large basement, garage, or workshop. In general, it’s wise to look for higher output when shopping for a garage heater that will be used to heat up a poorly insulated space. However, you may only need a unit that can heat 400–500 square feet if you’re simply trying to make a bedroom or dorm room toastier. Pro Tip

When shopping for any space heater, This Old House recommends opting for a unit with an output of 1,500 watts for room heating—less is really only suited for a desktop appliance. Key safety features to look for include a tip-over safety switch that automatically shuts off a toppled heater, overheat protection, and a UL or ETL stamp indicating the heater has been independently tested. Size

Size is not necessarily an indicator of heat output, but it is helpful in determining if a product will be a good fit for a space. Pay attention to both weight and dimensions. With some infrared heaters weighing over 25 pounds, you might be disappointed to learn that a design that looks compact may actually be very difficult to move from room to room. Some designs weigh just 4–7 pounds. Design

While most people think of a standard boxy design when picturing infrared space heaters, modern options often don’t even look like heaters at all. The “wheels” question is the first to check off your list. Some infrared space heaters are made with casters that allow them to be rolled easily from place to place. These casters also keep the heater slightly elevated off the floor. If you’re planning to use a single heater in various parts of the home throughout the day and night, these wheels can help you avoid scuffing your floors by dragging the unit. If you’re concerned about having a standalone infrared heater placed on the floor of your home due to pets, children, or a general lack of space, consider a wall-mounted version that resembles a mini-split HVAC system. Safety Features

People are sometimes reluctant to purchase space heaters because they have recollections of old, clunky space heaters from yesteryear that were hot to the touch, prone to overheating, and easy to tip over. The good news is that any new model of infrared space heater you’re purchasing today has tons of built-in safety features. Just be sure to confirm that a model has tip-over prevention, overheating prevention, and a frame that remains cool to the touch. It’s also helpful to select a model with a thermostat that shuts off automatically after a time to ensure that a forgotten space heater won’t be left on indefinitely. Pro Tip

An infrared space heater turns electricity into radiant heat. What makes infrared heat especially delightful is that its place on the electromagnetic spectrum causes it to produce a heat that’s similar to the heat you feel when you step into the warm sunshine. That’s why even people who have well-heated homes often prefer to introduce radiant heat for the purpose of creating a toasty, cozy feeling when relaxing or falling asleep.

Do Infrared Heaters Heat the Air?

No, infrared heaters don’t actually warm up the air. They warm up objects in a space. In general, infrared heaters are considered safer and more energy-efficient compared to traditional ceramic heating units. How long can you leave infrared heaters on?

Infrared heaters should never be left unattended for any reason. However, an infrared heater can essentially be left on indefinitely as long as it is being carefully monitored. The slow and steady buildup of heat generated by infrared heaters creates a very comfortable environment compared to the “stuffy” atmosphere that is often created by traditional air-warming space heaters.

Are infrared heaters good for electricity usage?

“Radiant heaters emit infrared radiation that directly heats objects and people within their line of sight, and are a more efficient choice when you will be in a room for only a few hours and can stay within the line of sight of the heater,” according to the U.S. Department of Energy. By converting nearly 100% of the electricity that they use to heat, infrared heaters offer an efficient way to heat a space.

What are some safety tips for using an infrared heater?

The top tip for safety when using infrared space heaters is to never leave a heater unattended while it is running. It’s also wise to place a heater a safe distance from furniture, walls, and other objects. Finally, be sure to test your household’s smoke and fire alarms regularly. If you’re purchasing an infrared heater that’s designed for outdoor use, be sure to utilize a ground fault circuit interrupter (GFCI) outlet that provides extra protection against electrical shock in environments where moisture might be present.
