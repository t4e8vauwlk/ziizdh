Over time, chainsaw blades become dull, making the tool less effective and potentially dangerous. Learning to sharpen your chainsaw can save you time and money while ensuring your equipment performs at its best. This Old House landscaping contractor Roger Cook demonstrates the proper technique for sharpening a chainsaw blade, and we’ll review them below. Understanding Chainsaw Sharpening

While there are services that will sharpen your chainsaw for you, it’s a good idea to at least understand why cutting with sharp blades is important and when to sharpen. Why Sharpen Your Chainsaw

A dull chainsaw blade can lead to the following issues. Inefficient cutting Increased fuel consumption Greater physical strain on the operator Higher risk of kickback and accidents

Unnecessary wear on the chainsaw’s engine and components

By keeping your chainsaw sharp, you ensure clean, fast cuts and reduce the risk of injury.

Pro Tip: Roger Cook, This Old House landscape contractor, stresses the importance of proactive maintenance: “This is a job you always wanna do before you need the piece of equipment. There’s nothing worse than having a limb or saw down and have to go in and sharpen it before you clean it up or cut firewood.” When To Sharpen Your Chainsaw

Whenever possible, you should sharpen the blades before you start to notice any of the following signs—once per year at the beginning of spring is usually sufficient. However, you’ll definitely know it’s time to stop and sharpen if any of these things happen.

The chainsaw produces sawdust instead of wood chips.

You need to apply increased pressure to cut through wood.

The chain pulls to one side while cutting.

You notice smoke while cutting, even with proper chain lubrication. The cut edges appear rough or uneven.

Pro Tip: Roger Cook, who has used a chain saw for over 20 years without an accident, follows a firm rule: never cut with a dull chain. Before firing up a saw, inspect for worn, chipped, or gouged teeth. One chain can last through about 20 sharpenings before it needs to be replaced.

Tools and Materials Needed To Sharpen a Chainsaw

Before you begin sharpening your chainsaw, gather the following tools and materials.

Round file (matching the diameter of your chain’s cutters) Flat file File guide or sharpening jig Depth gauge tool Work gloves Safety glasses

Owner’s manual for your specific chainsaw model

All these items can typically be found at your local home center or hardware store, or you can purchase them online as part of a chainsaw sharpening kit. Safety Precautions When Using Chainsaws

Safety should always be your top priority when working with chainsaws. Follow these precautions:

Ensure the chainsaw is turned off and cool before sharpening.

Wear work gloves to protect your hands from sharp edges.

Use safety glasses to shield your eyes from metal filings.

Follow these steps to sharpen your chainsaw effectively.

Clean the chain thoroughly to remove debris and oil.

Unless you have substantial experience with chainsaw sharpening, you’ll want to use a guide to maintain the correct angle while filing. These typically cost between $10 and $20 and are available online or in hardware stores. Here’s how to use one.

Select the appropriate file guide for your chain size.

Align the guide with the chain’s sharpening angle markings. Insert the round file into the guide.

Follow the contour of the cutter while keeping the guide flat against the chain.

Pro Tip: As Tim, a chainsaw maintenance expert featured on Ask This Old House, explained to Roger Cook about the file guide: “This sets through and rests on top of the tooth so as we file through, it keeps us from going too high or too low.” When Roger noticed the alignment markings on the guide, Tim added: “These actually help us to align that top angle. Keep it parallel here to the bar to keep that top angle correct.” Lowering the Rakers

The rakers, also known as depth gauges, are crucial for controlling the depth of each chainsaw cut. To maintain proper cutting performance, the rakers should be slightly shorter than the cutters. Here’s how to adjust the rakers to the correct height.

Use a depth gauge tool to check each raker’s height.

If the raker protrudes above the cutter, file it down using a flat file. File carefully to avoid removing too much material.

Check the height again with the depth gauge tool. Repeat for all rakers on the chain.

Pro Tip: As explained on a This Old House segment, the raker “actually guides the tooth straight through the wood, but it has to be a little bit lower than the tooth itself.” If it’s too high, “it’ll affect how the tooth cuts.” To file it down, simply use a flat file without hitting the tooth—the goal isn’t to sharpen the raker, but to lower it below the plane of the tooth.

Neglecting to sharpen all cutters equally Forgetting to adjust the rakers Maintaining Your Sharpened Chainsaw

After sharpening, proper maintenance is key to keeping your chainsaw in top condition.

Clean the chain thoroughly after each use. Keep the chain properly tensioned.

Ensure adequate lubrication during operation.

Store the chainsaw in a dry place when not in use.

Regularly inspect the chain for damage or wear. Additional Chainsaw Maintenance Tips

A well-maintained chainsaw is far safer and easier to use. In addition to sharpening, keep an eye on these features.

Inspect and clean the air filter: A clean air filter ensures efficient engine performance.

Check the spark plug: Periodically inspect and clean or replace the spark plug.

Tighten loose parts: Regularly check and tighten any loose screws or bolts to ensure the chainsaw operates smoothly.

Inspect the fuel system: Ensure there are no leaks and that the fuel mix is correct. Costs of Chainsaw Sharpening

Conducting your own chainsaw maintenance can save you money, but understanding potential costs is helpful.

DIY sharpening kits: Available for around $13–$40 and include files, a file guide, and a depth gauge. Some include a small rotary power tool.

Professional sharpening: Typically costs $12–$35 per chain.

Replacement chains: New chains can vary in price, generally costing between $9 and $43 depending on the size and brand. Environmental Impact and Recycling

Maintaining and sharpening your chainsaw also has environmental benefits. Obviously, sharpening a chain instead of buying a new one saves money and resources. It’s also true that properly sharpened tools work more efficiently, reducing fuel consumption and emissions.

When possible, consider recycling old chains and other metal parts to minimize environmental impact. Most hardware stores have recycling programs for tools and machinery parts.
