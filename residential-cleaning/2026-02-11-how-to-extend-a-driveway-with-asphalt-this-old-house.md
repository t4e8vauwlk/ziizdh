Careful planning is essential before breaking ground on your driveway extension. This stage involves several crucial steps to ensure a smooth and successful project. Obtaining Necessary Permissions

In many municipalities, the sidewalk and even part of your yard may belong to the town. Adding a parking space that affects town property requires permission. Check with your local authorities to determine if you need a permit for your driveway extension project. While this process is usually straightforward, it’s an essential step to avoid potential legal issues down the line. Choosing the Right Material

When extending your driveway, select a material that complements your existing surface and meets your needs.

Understanding the differences between asphalt versus concrete can help you make the best choice for your climate and budget needs.” Hiring a Professional Contractor

Once you’ve secured the necessary permissions and hired a contractor, it’s time to prepare for the actual work. This phase involves several critical steps to ensure a solid foundation for your new driveway extension. Marking Utilities

Before the excavation stage, it’s crucial to locate and mark all underground utilities. In the video, McCullough emphasizes the importance of this step to avoid damaging essential services.

Your contractor should contact utility locating services to identify and mark the following lines: Gas pipes Sewer connections Underground electrical cables Water lines

Knowing where these utilities are located helps prevent disruptions and ensures a safer work environment during excavation.

Pro Tip: The national 811 hotline makes this step easy. As This Old House Magazine has advised: “Punch in the three digits, and a utility crew shows up to map out the position of pipelines, wires, and other underground infrastructure free of charge.” It’s a simple call that can prevent a dangerous—and costly—accident. Installing the Asphalt Driveway Extension

With the groundwork prepared, it’s time to install the actual asphalt surface. This process involves multiple layers and specialized techniques to ensure a durable and long-lasting result. Apply the Base Layer

The first step in the asphalt installation process is applying the base layer. This layer consists of ¾-inch crushed stone, asphalt binder, and sand.

The base layer is spread evenly across the prepared area and then compacted using a roller. This layer provides the primary structure and support for your driveway extension.

Pro Tip: Roger Cook, This Old House landscape contractor, emphasizes that it’s “really important to get good compaction on our base.” On a recent This Old House project, the crew used 32 tons of asphalt for the base layer alone, followed by 34 tons of finish—a total of 66 tons for one large driveway. Add the Surface Layer

Once the base layer is in place and compacted, the surface layer is applied. This layer consists of ⅜-inch stone, asphalt binder, and additional sand.

The surface layer is carefully spread and shaped to ensure proper drainage and a smooth finish. Skilled workers use special boots and tools to work with the hot asphalt, which can reach temperatures between 200 and 300 degrees Fahrenheit. Compacting and Finishing

After both layers are in place, the contractor will use a roller to compact the entire surface. This process ensures a smooth, even finish and helps bond the layers together. Finally, a plate compactor may be used to address any remaining imperfections and create a polished look. Benefits of Asphalt Driveway Extensions

Pro Tip: Jim Capone, owner of a driveway sealing company in Yorktown Heights, New York, that seals more than 500 driveways annually, warns that oil stains are a sealer’s worst enemy: “If you try to apply sealer over oil stains left by cars, it will bubble up and peel right off like old paint.” He recommends using commercial degreasers but cautions homeowners to check the label and avoid products that contain “petroleum distillates,” since those contain the very substance you’re trying to keep off the driveway. Caring for Your New Driveway Extension

Proper care and maintenance will help ensure your new driveway extension lasts for years to come.

Here are some essential tips for maintaining your asphalt surface:

Allow the asphalt to cool and harden for at least four days before driving on it

Avoid parking heavy vehicles on the new extension for several weeks

Seal the asphalt after about six months to protect it from UV rays and moisture

Clean the surface regularly to prevent staining and damage

Address any cracks or damage promptly to prevent further deterioration

With proper care, your asphalt driveway extension can last up to 30 years, providing valuable additional space to your property. Regular Cleaning

Routine cleaning helps maintain the driveway’s appearance and prevents debris from causing damage. A simple sweep or rinsing with a hose can remove dirt and prevent stains. For tougher stains, a mild detergent or specialized asphalt cleaner can be used. Sealing the Driveway

Sealing the asphalt will help maintain its longevity. Depending on the local climate and usage, the sealing process should be done every two to three years. This protective layer acts as a barrier against UV rays, water, and chemicals.

Pro Tip: Kent Hansen, director of engineering at the National Asphalt Pavement Association, explains: “We believe it prevents driveway surfaces from oxidizing and becoming brittle. It also makes pavement look better.” Hansen adds that sealing every year is overkill, but once every three years or so should be adequate. Repairing Cracks Promptly

Learning how to repair asphalt potholes and cracks as soon as they appear prevents them from expanding and causing more significant damage. Small cracks can be filled with asphalt crack filler, while larger areas may require professional attention.
