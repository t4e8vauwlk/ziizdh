A circular saw is a valuable tool for DIY projects and professional woodworking. To get the best results and stay safe, it’s important to know how to adjust the blade depth properly. Setting the blade at the right depth helps you make cleaner cuts, keeps you safer, and extends the life of your saw. This Old House contributor Mark Clement demonstrates the proper technique for adjusting the blade depth on a circular saw in the Tool School above video. Why Blade Depth Matters

The depth of a circular saw blade plays a significant role in the quality of your cuts and the overall performance of the tool. It affects:

Cut quality: The right blade depth gives you cleaner, smoother cuts. If the blade is too deep, you can end up with rough edges or splintering. If it’s too shallow, the saw might not cut all the way through, which leads to uneven cuts.

Safety: A blade that’s set too far below the material being cut increases the risk of kickback, a dangerous situation where the saw can suddenly jerk back toward you. Setting the blade at the right depth helps prevent this and makes the saw easier to control.

Wear and tear: Adjusting the blade depth puts less stress on the saw’s motor and on the blade itself. This can extend the life of your tool and reduce the frequency of blade replacements, helping your saw run smoothly and efficiently.

To set the proper blade depth, unplug your saw and rest the base on the material you’re cutting with the blade next to the wood. Pull the guard back so you can see the blade’s teeth, then lock the saw at a depth that leaves the lowest tooth no more than about ¼ inch beneath the wood. This approach minimizes kickback and leaves fewer clawing teeth exposed beneath the cut. How To Adjust the Blade Depth

Adjusting the blade depth on your circular saw is simple. Here’s how to do it:

Unplug the saw or remove the battery for safety.

Find the blade depth adjustment lever or knob on your saw.

Loosen the lever or knob to allow the baseplate (also called the shoe) to move freely.

Place the saw on the material you intend to cut.

Adjust the blade so it extends about 1/8–1/4 inch below the bottom of the material.

Tighten the lever or knob to lock the blade depth in place.

Clement recommends a trick for finding the correct depth: place your finger under the baseplate while holding the saw up, then tighten the lever. This method helps you get a consistent depth every time. Tips for Using a Circular Saw

To get the most out of your circular saw, keep these additional tips in mind:

Always wear safety glasses and hearing protection when operating the saw.

Keep the baseplate clean and free of debris for smooth operation.

Make sure the blade guard is functioning correctly before each use.

Support the material properly to prevent binding or pinching of the blade.

Use a sharp blade appropriate for the material you’re cutting.

Pro Tip: Tom Silva, This Old House general contractor, warns that when a motor slows down, “the blade heats up and dulls quickly.” This not only produces a poor cut, it’s dangerous because the blade can climb out of the kerf and push the saw back toward the user. If you notice the motor bogging down, stop and let it recover or switch to a sharper blade.

According to Norm Abram’s circular saw guide in This Old House Magazine, you should support the material on a bench or two strong sawhorses, with enough overhang so the cut piece falls away freely. Never prop up the offcut, or the material will buckle and bind the blade, causing dangerous kickback. Common Mistakes To Avoid

When adjusting and using a circular saw, be aware of these common pitfalls:

Forcing the saw: Let the saw do the work. Pushing too hard can lead to inaccurate cuts and potentially damage the tool or material. The saw is designed to cut smoothly with minimal force if used correctly.

Neglecting to check the depth: Always verify the blade depth before starting a cut, especially if you’ve been using the saw for different tasks or materials. Inconsistent depth settings can drastically affect cut quality and tool safety.

Setting the blade too deep: A blade that extends too far below the material can cause splintering, increase the risk of kickback, and put unnecessary strain on the saw’s motor. Avoid this by sticking faithfully to the recommended depth settings. Maintaining Your Circular Saw

Regular maintenance can help your circular saw perform at its best:

Tighten any loose screws or bolts regularly.

Clean the saw after every use, especially around the blade and vents, to keep it running smoothly.

Lubricate moving parts as directed by the manufacturer.

Replace worn or damaged blades as soon as you notice a problem.

Store the saw in a dry place to avoid rust or damage.

Pro Tip: Norm Abram, This Old House master carpenter, advises listening to your saw as part of routine maintenance: “If your saw doesn’t sound right, shut it off and find the problem. It could be a dull or dirty blade.” He also recommends sighting down the baseplate periodically to confirm it’s square and parallel to the blade, especially if your cuts aren’t coming out square or the saw is binding. Other Uses for Your Circular Saw

Circular saws are versatile tools. Beyond simple cuts, you can use them for beveling, plunge cuts, and dado cuts by adjusting angles and depths appropriately. Make sure your saw is capable of such tasks and understand the specific adjustment mechanisms involved. Always refer to the manufacturer’s manual for particular model capabilities and limitations.

Understanding proper blade depth adjustment is just one aspect of mastering circular saw use. Familiarize yourself with various blades and their purposes.
