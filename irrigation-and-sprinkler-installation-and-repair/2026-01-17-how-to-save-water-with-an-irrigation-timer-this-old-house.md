Watering your lawn and garden can be a tricky balance—you want to keep your plants healthy, but you don’t want to waste water. That’s where an irrigation timer comes in. These handy devices automate your sprinkler system so your landscaping gets the right amount of water without overwatering.

In this guide, we’ll explore various types of irrigation timers and how they can help you save water while keeping your landscape healthy with the help of This Old House landscape contractor Roger Cook. Manual Irrigation Timers

Manual timers are the simplest and most affordable option for controlling your irrigation system. These devices attach directly to your outdoor faucet. You just set the watering duration, turn on the water, and the timer will automatically shut it off when the time is up.

The timer will automatically shut off the water flow after the set duration, preventing overwatering and waste. To use a manual timer, follow these five steps:

Attach the timer to your outdoor faucet. Connect your garden hose to the timer.

Install a sprinkler at the end of the hose.

Set the desired watering duration (up to 45 minutes). Turn on the water.

Battery-powered timers have more advanced features than manual timers, but they’re still relatively simple to use. These devices provide greater control over your watering schedule by offering you programmable and easy-to-adjust irrigation times. Many have additional features like multiple watering zones and rain sensors to stop unnecessary irrigation. Installation and Programming

Installing a battery-powered timer is straightforward. Here’s what the process looks like:

Attach the timer to your outdoor faucet. Connect your garden hose and sprinkler.

Insert batteries according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Program the desired start time, frequency, and duration of watering.

Once programmed, the timer will automatically control your sprinkler system according to your settings.

Additional Tips for Battery-Powered Timers

To get the best performance from your battery-powered timer, check and replace the batteries often, keep it clean and dry to prevent dust and water damage, and test it periodically.

Tips for Maximizing Water Savings

No matter which type of timer you choose, there are several ways to maximize its water-saving potential:

Adjust your watering schedule seasonally to match plant growth cycles and weather changes.

Choose drought-resistant plants for your landscape to minimize watering needs.

Regularly maintain and inspect your irrigation system for leaks and inefficiencies.

Use mulch to retain soil moisture and reduce evaporation, enhancing plant health.

Water early in the morning to reduce evaporation and help plants absorb moisture.

Pro Tip: Automatic watering controllers aren’t smart enough to come in out of the rain on their own.
