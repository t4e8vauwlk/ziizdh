The condition of the wall where the bay window will be installed directly impacts the window’s performance. Sometimes, structural issues are not apparent until the old window is removed and the contractor gets a good look inside the wall. Repairs need to be completed before a new window can be installed, which can drive up the project’s cost. Some walls can be opened up with no issues for a new bay window installation, but load-bearing walls and studs may need to be moved. The price range to open up a load-bearing wall runs from $500 to $3,000, depending on the location and the project’s overall scope. Repairing drywall, trim, and siding can also increase bay window installation cost by $300 to $1,000 or more. Insulation and Waterproofing

Weatherproofing and insulation requires the positioning of insulation in the spaces around the window. This can cost an additional $450 to $3,000. Here are the different types of bay windows and their average prices. Bow

Bow windows don’t protrude as far away from the wall of a house as much as a bay window. A bow window usually has between four and six glass panels that are set at a wider angle than the ones in a bay window. Incorporating additional glass panels provides a gentle curve to the window. Bow windows can cost 1.5 to 2.5 times more than a bay window, and new bow windows typically run between $3,000 to $10,000 per window. Box Bay

Box bay windows look like half of a box, with three glass panes at 90-degree angles. This type of bay window is usually small, typically 3 by 3 feet or 4 by 4 feet. They’re commonly found in kitchens and are cheaper to install than other types of bay windows. This popular style needs to be custom fit, and they cost between $500 to $900 for materials. Circle Bay

This high-end bay window option has larger panes of glass that are fully curved to create a half-circle. They usually have ornamental details and intricate designs. The cost of a circle bay window is between $2,000 and $3,000. Full Bay

Full bay windows are larger than box bay windows since they’re wider and taller. Also known as a canted window, it has three panes of glass: the middle one is flat and the other two are angled at either 30 or 45 degrees. Full bay windows are only installed on the first floor of a home and they can cost between $800 and $2,100 for materials. Oriel

First popularized during the Renaissance era, this type of window is the oldest bay window style. Oriel windows are built into the side of a home and can be installed on any level. Structural support is supplied with a decorative corbel or bracket under the window. The cost of materials for an oriel bay window averages $900 to $2,500.
